First time you open it

The first time you open pgpilot, a tutorial takes you through it. Four quick questions: what you fly, what kind of flying, where your phone sits, and whether you have your own vario. The answers pick your instrument layout and whether pgpilot beeps as your vario.

Click. Then only the permissions your phone needs to record a flight, one at a time, each with a reason: location, and on Android also always-on location, notifications and battery. The microphone is asked the first time you talk.

Then a short tour of your screen: Plan and Active, the instruments, comms and push to talk, map settings.

Click. Everything is in Menu, Tutorials, under Help. Do them in any order, come back any time. Do “Get ready to fly” now if you skipped it.

Our game for the season here. Hanuman is lying along the Dhauladhar, from Dharamshala to Joginder Nagar, about 740 km2. He has lost the herb, and you help him look for it.

Every time you fly over a patch inside him, you search it once. Whoever has searched a patch most often holds it; if two are level, nobody does. Flying straight searches a band either side of your track, wider the further you get from takeoff. Close a loop and you take everything inside it.

9 October to 15 November. Whoever holds the most ground at the end of 15 November wins a reward. Free, automatic: every flight in the app counts, and flights from XContest and Flightlog too. It rescores every hour.

Click for the board. (Snapshot from this afternoon, open pgpilot.app/bir for the live one.) And as the poster says: fly within your limits, no herb is worth a risk.

The Bir pack: three radios

VHF

Voice, when nothing else works. A KV4P radio straight on the phone.

LoRa

Position and short text with no infrastructure. FANET and Meshtastic.

Satellite

When there's no coverage at all. InReach.

The part that matters here. Behind the first ridge the internet is gone, the landings have no road out, and nobody is on call. That’s why this valley runs its own radios, and why the app talks to all three.

VHF on the phone: KV4P Beta

  • VHF radio straight into pgpilot over Bluetooth
  • Sends both to the app and on VHF
  • Works as a repeater between app and radio

Still beta. Pair it under Menu, Hardware. When you talk, it goes out in the pgpilot group and on VHF at once, so friends on either hear you. It can relay between the two.

Meshtastic, Bircom and FANET

  • Meshtastic and FANET radios straight into pgpilot over Bluetooth
  • Status and position over Bircom and FANET
  • Messages over Bircom, no cell signal needed
  • Works with pgpilot ground contacts: push and email to friends when you take off and when you've landed well

Most of you already fly with a Meshtastic radio for Bircom. Pair it in pgpilot and your status, position and messages go over the mesh, no coverage needed. FANET trackers too. And it works with ground contacts: the friends you added get a push or an email when you take off and when you’ve landed well. Close the Meshtastic app first: the radio takes one Bluetooth client at a time.

Buying a radio: a T-Echo or a SenseCAP T1000-E. Pick 868 MHz in India, the listings default to 915 or 433.

InReach

Garmin InReach MapShare

Under Connected services you enter your MapShare name, the part after share.garmin.com/, once.

Inbound, not outbound

The app pulls your satellite position into pgpilot. It is not a channel we transmit to, and not messages or SOS.

For the others

Your position lands on everyone else's map even where there's no cell coverage at all, and someone at home can follow the dot.

Say it right: inbound only. Enter the MapShare name once, and the others see you where nothing else gets through. Not SOS.
